1. The Map (What you see)
The global map you see in the mobile app usually comes from a "Tile Provider" (like Mapbox, Google Maps, or OpenStreetMap). These are just images (tiles) loaded from the internet. That's why the map looks fine even if your backend is having trouble.

2. OSRM (The "Brain" for Routing)
Your local OSRM instance is the "brain" that does the heavy lifting for your ride-hailing logic. Here is what happens when those Iraq map files are missing:

Inaccurate Prices (Fare Estimation): I checked your trips.service.ts code. Right now, if OSRM is down, it falls back to a "straight-line" calculation. This is much less accurate because it doesn't know about one-way streets, dead ends, or actual road turns. It just guesses based on the distance "as the crow flies."
No Travel Time Estimates: Without OSRM, the app can't accurately tell a rider: "Your driver is 5 minutes away." It has to guess, which leads to a poor user experience.
Incorrect Path Plotting: When a trip starts, the blue line that follows the streets from the pickup point to the destination is generated by OSRM. Without these files, that line might just be a straight line through buildings, or it might not show up at all.
Driver Matching: In a real-world scenario, you want to match the rider with the driver who is closest by driving time, not just physical distance. OSRM provides the driving matrix for this.
In summary: The app looks good because the "wallpaper" (the map tiles) is working, but the "business engine" (pricing, timing, and routing) needs those Iraq files to function correctly for your specific region.
